Lore Foundation

The Gatekeeper, The Judge Between Realms

Crosses between Living and Dead once per round, gaining power whenever he or his followers change realms.

Unit Role

Holder of the Vacant Office

A soul comes forward. I hear it. I weigh it. I decide whether the door remembers how to open.

The first thing taken from the Gatekeeper is his name. The contract lies open on an onyx table between two realms, its final line waiting beneath a black seal. The man who signs it remembers having lived but cannot recover the word by which the Living called him. Ink passes through the place where that name should be. Scribe's books preserve a blank line. Even Echo can repeat only the silence left behind. For now, and perhaps for longer than now, he is simply the Gatekeeper.

The office is older than its holder. At the Threshold, a queue of souls gathers before doors that do not open onto places so much as verdicts. Each soul comes forward. He hears it, weighs it and decides: pass, hold or refuse. The dead may plead honestly, lie beautifully or offer Tokens made by dead hands - coins, teeth, rings, promises hardened into objects. Accepting such an offering may fund the binding of a violent spirit or the sealing of a failing Gate. It may also make judgement look uncomfortably like trade. The office provides rules, but not innocence.

Sebastien serves as Steward of the Threshold and clerk of impossible cases. Slender, immaculate and patient, he knows where the ledgers are kept, which doors must never be opened and how previous Gatekeepers failed. He answers the question asked rather than the one meant, a habit that makes his assistance both indispensable and suspect. Whether he serves the office, the Gate or some design of his own is a distinction he has never volunteered.

Unlike Archimedes and Balthazar, the Gatekeeper belongs wholly to neither side of the divide. In the Living Realm he is recognisably a man: breathing, woundable and burdened by instincts that survive the loss of biography. In the Dead Realm his outline loosens. Colour drains from him; light shows through old injuries; voices waiting at the Gate can be heard within his shadow. Yet he is not dead. He may cross back, cheating the finality that governs ordinary souls.

Every passage alters him. Power gathers not through conquest but through traffic: a soul judged, an ally crossing, the Gatekeeper himself stepping from breath into pallor and back again. In battle this makes him initially slight beside the archangel and archdemon. Over time, however, each transition becomes precedent, and precedent is the oldest magic of any threshold. The more often the boundary acknowledges his authority, the harder he becomes to deny.

That authority carries a private danger. A Gate is not merely a door but a pressure held in shape. Violent Undead can assault it. Too many false judgements can make it unstable. If the Threshold breaks, the distinction between Living and Dead ceases to be a law and becomes a suggestion. The Gatekeeper must therefore judge individuals while also preserving the system that makes judgement possible. Mercy toward one soul may endanger a thousand. Refusal may keep the realms safe and still be wrong.

He has begun to suspect that his missing name was not taken as payment, but removed as evidence. Certain souls recognise his face and recoil. Others address him with titles that Sebastien insists are obsolete. At rare moments the Gates respond to his anger before he has consciously commanded them. The deeper nature of the office remains unrevealed, as does the reason a seemingly ordinary man can inhabit it. The Gatekeeper's central mystery is not whether he possesses power. It is what kind of being must forget itself in order to judge others fairly.
